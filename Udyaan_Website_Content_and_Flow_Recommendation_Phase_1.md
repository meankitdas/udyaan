01 — EXECUTIVE DIRECTION

# What we are changing

The current website is well designed at an editorial level, but its information architecture makes Udyaan feel primarily like a high-end internship / living-lab programme. The first screen says “living lab,” then the site moves through benefits, field systems, projects, selection, the four-week sprint, disciplines, stipend and FAQ. That order is coherent for a programme brochure, but it is not the strongest order for an incubation / launchpad platform.

**Current-site evidence:** the live site currently presents the model, projects, journey, drone simulator, contact/sign-in and apply actions in the main navigation; the hero leads with the “living lab” concept and immediately moves to food/water/energy/waste. \[Current website, accessed Sep 2026\]

03 — WHY THE CURRENT CONTENT ARCHITECTURE UNDERPERFORMS

# The issue is not the quality of the writing. The issue is the order of meaning.

## A. The product is introduced as a place, not a platform.

“Living lab,” farm, systems and zones appear early. A launchpad site normally needs to establish the operating model — what enters, what happens, and what comes out — before going deep on facilities.

## B. The student programme is more visible than the venture engine.

Selection, weeks, discipline lists and benefits are easy to understand, but the company -\> problem -\> student -\> solution -\> adoption pathway is comparatively under-emphasised on the live site.

## C. “Project” is doing too much work.

The current project section names projects, but a launchpad should make the problem itself discoverable. The problem statement should be the primary object of interaction.

## D. Outcomes are presented as benefits rather than progression.

MVP, portfolio, mentorship, TBI access and investor readiness appear as programme benefits. They should instead be positioned as stages/resources in a venture progression.

## E. The two-sided marketplace is hidden.

The investor deck shows company adoption as a key economic outcome. The public site currently does not give “For Companies / Bring a Problem” equal structural weight to “For Students.”

## F. Proof arrives too late or is too abstract.

The site says “Build the system. Prove the value.” but the future website should show actual problem briefs, solution evidence, adoption stories and venture outcomes as first-class content.

# What strong launchpad / incubator / venture-platform sites consistently do

The external benchmark is being used for architecture, not for copying language. The strongest relevant patterns observed across university launchpads, accelerators, incubators, venture studios and VC-style platforms are:

| **Benchmark pattern**               | **Website implication for Udyaan**                                                                           |
|-------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Make the thesis legible immediately | A strong statement of what the platform believes and what it enables.                                        |
| Show the mechanism                  | Visitors can see how people, problems, mentors, capital and outcomes connect.                                |
| Separate audiences                  | Students/founders and companies/partners get clear pathways.                                                 |
| Make the opportunity browsable      | Problem boards, projects, ventures or portfolio objects are presented as things a user can explore.          |
| Show progression                    | The site explains the stages from application or problem intake to build, validation and next stage.         |
| Use proof as evidence               | Portfolios, outcomes, founder stories and measurable results reduce the need for generic marketing copy.     |
| Keep the CTA close to intent        | Apply, submit a problem, explore a venture, or partner should appear immediately after the relevant content. |

07 — DETAILED CONTENT REQUIREMENTS FOR EACH SECTION

## 01. HERO

The hero must answer three questions immediately: What is Udyaan? What can I do here? Where can this lead? Keep the headline short. Use one support line. Use two CTAs: Explore Problems and Join Udyaan. Avoid explaining the complete programme in the hero.

## 02. THESIS

Explain the strategic reason for the platform. The core idea is not “students need internships.” It is “real problems and capable builders are often separated.” Udyaan creates a structured path between them. This is where the philosophical language belongs.

## 03. THE MODEL

Use a large diagram, not paragraphs. The content should say that a problem can enter from a company/industry or another validated source; students are matched/selected; the team builds with support; the solution is tested; evidence determines the next move; adoption or venture creation can follow.

## 04. PROBLEMS

This is a core product surface. A problem card should be more useful than a project poster. Recommended metadata: source, category, status, challenge type, skills required, stage. The user should be able to click into the full brief.

## 05. PROBLEM DETAIL

Every problem needs a consistent template. Do not allow long prose to become the interface. Use sections: Context; Problem; User; Why now; Constraints; What is being asked; Success criteria; Skills needed; Available support; Submission/apply action.

## 06. WHO CAN BUILD

Use capability language first, degree language second. The core sentence can be “The problem decides who needs to be in the room.” Then show capability groups and examples of contribution.

## 07. SELECTION

The selection page should reveal what gets evaluated and why. Keep the current rigor, but make the purpose explicit: selecting people who can reason, execute, collaborate and adapt.

## 08. BUILD JOURNEY

Use a stage-based flow. The current four-week content can remain as the programme timeline, but the higher-level experience should be Understand -\> Research -\> Frame -\> Build -\> Test -\> Iterate. This makes the platform feel like a product-building system.

## 09. COMPANY + MENTOR LOOP

Show the feedback loop as an interaction, not as a mentor benefits card. The problem owner helps define the context, the student team builds, mentors guide, and the company/user side provides validation.

## 10. VALIDATION

Introduce evidence gates. This is the point where the website should explain that a prototype is not automatically a venture. The solution needs evidence of technical feasibility, user value and commercial viability before moving to the next stage.

## 11. OUTCOMES

Use a decision-tree structure: company adoption, research/IP, or venture creation. This creates credibility because it does not overpromise that every student project becomes a startup.

## 12. VENTURE PATH

This is the launchpad identity. Show a clear progression from problem to venture. Keep the language practical and avoid calling the university a VC fund unless it actually invests capital.

## 13. FOUNDER PATH

Explain that founder status can emerge from execution. The user can enter as a student, become the person who understands the problem, own/continue the solution, and potentially become a solo founder or co-founder subject to the actual IP/ownership model.

## 14. SUPPORT SYSTEM

Group support by stage: technical build; domain expertise; product; business; legal/IP; incubation; funding introductions. Avoid a flat logo wall without context.

## 15. VENTURES / PORTFOLIO

Once the platform has outcomes, the page should behave like a living portfolio. Each record should tell a repeatable story: problem, builder, build, validation, outcome, current stage.

## 16. FOR COMPANIES

Make it easy for a company to understand what it gets by bringing a problem. The company should see a clear intake path and what happens after submission. This is essential to the platform’s supply side.

## 17. FOR STUDENTS

Make this the cleanest student route. Explain who it is for, what happens, what is expected, what the student leaves with, and how a solution can continue.

## 18. ABOUT / ECOSYSTEM

Place institutional credibility after product clarity. Explain who operates the platform, what university infrastructure is connected, and who the ecosystem includes.

## 19. FAQ

Prioritise the questions that a real platform user would ask: who owns the IP, who can submit a problem, how teams form, what happens after validation, how adoption works, whether a student can continue the venture, and what support exists after the programme.

## 20. FINAL CTA

Final CTA should be based on intent. “Explore Problems” is for people who want to discover an opportunity. “Join Udyaan” is for students/builders. “Bring a Problem” is for companies. Do not make every visitor choose the same form.

11 — CONTENT RULES FOR THE REWRITE

# How the final copy should sound

- Professional and confident, but not corporate-jargon-heavy.

- Use short, declarative headlines; use supporting copy to explain rather than headline stacks.

- Describe mechanisms before benefits. Explain what happens before listing what the student gets.

- Use “problem,” “solution,” “build,” “validate,” “adoption,” “venture,” and “founder” consistently.

- Do not promise venture creation as an automatic outcome. Present it as a possible path when the solution earns the right to continue.

- Use proof wherever possible: real problem briefs, outcomes, company participation, validation evidence and venture stories.

- Keep investor-only material out of the public flow unless it directly builds trust or explains the venture mechanism.
